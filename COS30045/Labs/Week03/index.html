COS30045 / EXERCISE 3.0 Data & method

An Australian television data story

Bigger screens come with bigger energy figures.

A closer look at how screen diagonal relates to the annual energy consumption printed on television labels.

4,508 records
Available television registration records that list Australia as a market in the supplied course dataset.

01 / Setting

Most records fall into the middle size ranges.

The 60–69 inch range contains the most records in this dataset. Screens 90 inches or larger appear much less often.

This matters because the mix of screen sizes affects comparisons across television models.

02 / Finding

The median annual energy figure rises with size.

Median labelled consumption rises from 116 kWh/year for screens below 40 inches to 1,058 kWh/year for screens of 90 inches or more.

Bigger screens are associated with higher energy figures in these records.

03 / Context

Technology is only part of the explanation.

Among 60–69 inch records, the medians are 405 kWh/year for LCD, 465 for LCD (LED), and 451 for OLED.

These differences are descriptive. They do not prove that one screen technology causes higher energy use.

04 / Takeaway

Compare size first, then read the label.

Screen size is a useful starting point when comparing televisions. It is still worth checking the energy label of the individual model rather than making a decision from screen technology alone.

These rows describe television registrations, not unit sales or household electricity bills. The labelled consumption figure is not a measurement of any one household's actual use.

Data & method. I used the supplied tv_2026_02_15.csv, kept rows marked Available and listed for sale in Australia, converted centimetres to inches, and calculated registration counts and median labelled kWh/year by size group. The script and summary data are included with the project.
