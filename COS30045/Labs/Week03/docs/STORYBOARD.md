# Storyboard - TV size and annual energy labels

## Audience and purpose

I aimed this page at readers who compare TVs using specifications such as screen size and screen technology, but who may not know how to interpret annual energy labels.

The main message is that screen diagonal is closely related to labelled energy consumption. Technology matters, but the category name is not enough for a reliable conclusion about any individual model.

## Story sequence

| Part | Reader's question | Visual | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Setting | Which screen sizes are represented? | Bar chart of size-band counts | Establish the composition of the data |
| Finding | What happens to labelled consumption as size rises? | Line chart of size-band medians | Show the main relationship |
| Context | Does technology explain everything? | Horizontal bars for 60-69 inch TVs | Compare technologies while holding size range roughly constant |
| Takeaway | What should I look at when comparing TVs? | Short text summary | Connect the finding to reading energy labels |

## Design choices

- I converted screen diagonal from centimetres into inches because TV sizes are commonly discussed in inches.
- I used consistent ten-inch categories and direct labels to make the charts easy to read.
- I used the median rather than the mean, which reduces the influence of unusually high consumption figures.
- I chose the 60-69 inch band because LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED all appear in it with a usable number of records.
- I kept this as a static explanatory story, not a dashboard with filters, because the Week 3 focus is communication.

## Interpretation and limits

Registration records are not sales figures. The annual figures come from energy labels, not actual power bills. Differences between technologies in one size band are descriptive and should not be treated as proof of cause and effect.
